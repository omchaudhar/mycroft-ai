# Pitch deck — speaker-ready content

15 slides. Each has a **headline** (what goes on the slide), **on-slide**
content, and **say** (what the presenter says). Every figure is generated by
the prototype and lives in `outputs/`.

Target: 8 minutes of talking, 4 minutes of live demo, the rest for questions.

---

## 1 — Title

**Mycroft.ai**
*A configurable Responsible AI control plane for the enterprise AI portfolio*

On-slide: Team Mycroft.ai · IIT Bombay · Track 1 (ControlPlane.ai)
Say: "Enterprises don't have an AI system. They have a portfolio of them. That
is the whole problem."

---

## 2 — The problem is the portfolio, not the model

On-slide: three columns — customer support assistant (external, 100 ms budget,
high blast radius) · internal copilot (employees, tolerant, noisy sources) ·
decision support (regulated, a decision about a person).

Say: "One checker across all three is too slow for the first, too noisy for
the second, and too shallow for the third. The risk signature is different in
each column, and so is the acceptable cost of getting it wrong."

---

## 3 — Four things that make this genuinely hard

On-slide:
- The risk families **overlap** — a fabricated detail about a person is a hallucination *and* a privacy leak
- There is **no real-time ground truth** — the gap that causes the hallucination also blocks its verification
- **Over-flagging** kills adoption; **under-flagging** creates liability. There is no setting that avoids both
- **Regulation varies by geography and moves** — DPDP, EU AI Act, US sectoral

Say: "We didn't design around these. We designed for them, and I'll show you
each one working."

---

## 4 — Core insight

On-slide, large: **AI governance cannot be one-size-fits-all — so make it configuration.**

`Jurisdiction → Policy Pack → Evaluation Contract → Detector configuration`

Say: "Regulatory requirements are policy configuration, not application logic.
If the EU AI Act changes in August, that is a YAML file, not a release."

---

## 5 — Architecture

On-slide: the two-lane diagram from `docs/METHODOLOGY.md`.

Say: "Fast Lane is deterministic, inline, on 100% of traffic — PII with real
checksum validation, deny lists, loop breakers. Measured p95 is under a twentieth of a
millisecond of compute, against a 100 millisecond budget. Slow Lane is everything semantic;
it runs asynchronously and never sits between the user and their response.
That split isn't an optimisation, it's the only way to have both."

---

## 6 — The Evaluation Contract is the product

On-slide: a real contract YAML, and beneath it the merge result for IN vs EU.

Say: "Human-approved. Content-hashed — `customer_support@r2-5ae442dedc`. Diffable

lower it. A team cannot configure its way out of a regulatory floor."

## 8 — Hallucination, privacy, bias — and the overlap

On-slide: the overlap case. Response: *"Your account manager is Priya Nair,
reach her on +91 98330 11477."* Two labels, one action.

Say: "We resolve the overlap with evidence, not wording. Personal data that
appears in the retrieved context is real data being disclosed — a leak.
Personal data that appears nowhere in context was invented — a leak *and* a
fabrication, and strictly worse, because there is no source to correct. Both
labels are recorded. One action is taken: the strictest one any finding
demands."

---

## 9 — Bias: a screening signal, not a verdict

On-slide: counterfactual table — same application, `gender` swapped,
recommendation flips approve → decline, confidence 0.88 → 0.55.

Say: "We hold every permitted factor constant, swap one protected attribute,
and compare. A flip, a confidence move beyond the noise floor, or proxy
language appearing — 'profile', 'less typical' — is a signal that this case
needs a human. It is **not** a finding of discrimination, and we say so on
every screen that shows it. One trace can never establish disparate treatment."

---

## 10 — Measured, including where we lose

On-slide, both tables from `outputs/metrics.md`.

Generated corpus (264): privacy 0.957/1.000 · hallucination 0.965/0.887 ·
bias 1.000/1.000 · policy 0.800/1.000
Hand-written holdout (30): privacy 1.00/1.00 · policy 1.00/1.00 ·
**hallucination 0.857 / 0.750**

Say: "The holdout was written by hand *after* the detectors, to test the risk
rather than the templates. Hallucination recall drops to 0.75 there. That gap
is real and it is one specific class: open-ended fabrication with no figure to
contradict — an invented loyalty tier. Our offline judge is built around
figures. That's exactly what the LLM-judge adapter is for, and it ships in the
repo. We'd rather show you the hole than have you find it."

## 11 — The trade-off, priced before you sign

On-slide: the customer_support/hallucination threshold sweep.

Say: "Over-flagging causes alert fatigue, under-flagging causes liability, and
no threshold avoids both. So we don't pretend to solve it — we price it. Here
is what each sensitivity catches and what it costs, measured, before a contract
is agreed. A high-risk decision tool buys fewer misses with more false
positives. An internal assistant makes the opposite trade on purpose."

## 12 — From findings to a closable defect

On-slide: **DEF-EA14BE** — "Response restates a governed figure incorrectly" ·
16 occurrences in 114 traces · root cause: system prompt does not require the
answer to quote the retrieved policy · **≈ Rs 3.55 lakh/month**, with the
formula printed underneath.

Say: "Sixteen traces, one defect, one owner, one root cause. And the price is
not a number we made up — it's the measured occurrence rate times assumptions
that are printed right there and that you can change. If you think a handoff
costs Rs 200, not Rs 95, change one line and the number moves."

---

## 13 — Closing the loop — and what it cost

On-slide, from `outputs/before_after.md`:

| | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Contract | `customer_support@r1-b7cc3c4d4f` | `customer_support@r2-5ae442dedc` |
| Reaching the user | 6 | **3** |
| False positives | 2 | 13 |
| Human review load | 60 / 114 | 64 / 114 |

Say: "A reviewer confirms the defect. That produces a versioned contract
change. We re-run the defect's own traces plus every trace a reviewer has
since confirmed shows the same failure — and failures reaching the user halve.
It costs eleven false positives and four extra reviews out of a hundred and
fourteen. In Round 1 we had a −94% on this slide. It wasn't measured, so it's
gone. This one is, and it comes with its bill."

## 14 — Business model and roadmap

On-slide: buyer map · $30k–150k ACV by governed interaction volume · four
phases from read-only ingestion to portfolio governance.

Say: "We start read-only against the tracing layer they already run. Nothing
inline, nothing blocked, first defect register in weeks. Inline Fast Lane only
after the contracts are signed off by their risk team."

---

## 15 — Risks, and the close

On-slide: the risk table's top three — detection commoditises (we don't sell
detection); alert fatigue (measured per threshold); the bias screen being
over-read (framed as screening everywhere).

Closing line: **"Turn every AI failure into a better control — and be able to
prove it held."**

Say: "Anyone can flag a PII leak. Almost nobody turns forty of them into one
owned, priced defect, carries a human's decision into a versioned contract, and
then proves with a regression run that the fix worked — and tells you what it
cost. That's the gap, and that's what's running in this repo."
